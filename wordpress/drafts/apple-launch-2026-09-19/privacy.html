Spotter Privacy Policy

Last updated 20 September 2026

Spotter is operated by Quarterdeck Collective LLC. This page says plainly what it stores and what it does not do. If anything here is unclear, email business@quarterdeckcollective.com.

What Spotter stores

  • Your email address and password. Handled by Supabase Auth; the password is stored only as a salted hash and is never visible to us.
  • If you continue with Google or Apple, Spotter receives the account identifier, name and email address the provider shares, together with tokens needed to authenticate the connection. Your provider password never reaches Spotter. Apple lets you hide your real email address behind a private relay. For Apple sign-in, Spotter also keeps a refresh token in server-only storage so it can revoke your Apple authorization when you delete your account.
  • The videos you save — the link, the creator name, the caption or description that was public on the post, and the workout extracted from it.
  • What you log — the sets, reps and weights you record in Workout Mode, plus any notes and planned days you add.
  • Your preferences and permission choices, including the date and version of your AI processing choice and optional reminder settings.
  • A personal save key so the iPhone share extension or an iOS Shortcut can add videos for you. You can replace it any time from Settings.
  • Your conversations with Pumpy, the coach, so you can pick a chat up where you left it, and the changes you confirmed from them. They are private to your account.
  • If you subscribe to Spotter Plus, an identifier for you at your payment provider and the state of your subscription — the plan, whether it is running, and the date it renews or ends. Never your card. See Payments below.

What Spotter does not do

  • No ads or advertising trackers, and no third-party web analytics scripts. Spotter does keep the operational and subscription measurements described below.
  • Your library, logs and plan are private to your account and are not sold. Relevant details are processed by the service providers described below when you use AI features.
  • Spotter does not collect your location. Workout logs are fitness information, and anything you type into notes or Pumpy may include health or other sensitive information. Optional Apple Watch heart-rate access is described below.

Other services involved

Spotter asks for permission before using AI features. You can decline and keep logging workouts and planning manually. In Settings, you can review or turn off AI processing. Turning it off stops future AI requests after the choice is saved; it does not recall requests already sent or delete saved workouts and conversations.

Saving a post can send its caption, description, carousel images, or text you supply to OpenAI to extract exercises. Google Gemini is used for supported audio/video reads, including sampled video frames. Text, coaching and image requests do not automatically fall back to Google. Private uploads can also be processed by Google. Groq is no longer part of the active reading workflow.

Asking Pumpy, tapping How to do this, or Swap or modify also sends an AI model what you typed plus what it needs to answer: the titles, exercises, equipment and collection names of the workouts you have saved, your planned days, and a summary of your recent sessions (how many, which muscles, how much volume). Pumpy sends a short index of that — titles, categories, equipment, collection names, your planned days — at the start of every message so it needs fewer round trips, and sends a workout's exercises only when it needs them to answer. Account email is not deliberately included in these requests. Text you put in a message, exercise detail or selected workout may be included, so avoid adding sensitive information unnecessarily. Pumpy cannot change anything in your library until you confirm it in the app.

Data is stored with Supabase in the United States. Fonts are loaded from Google Fonts.

Video playback happens in an embedded player from Instagram, TikTok or YouTube. Those players are the platforms' own and follow the platforms' privacy policies.

When you ask how an exercise is done, Spotter shows a short demonstration clip. These come from a short list of fitness creators' public YouTube channels, collected in advance and stored by exercise, so most of the time nothing is looked up at all — the answer is already in Spotter's own list, and more than one creator may be offered for the same movement. For a movement that list does not cover, the server falls back to searching YouTube, preferring those same channels. Either way only the name of the exercise is sent — never your email, your account or anything else about you — and any search result is cached and shared by everyone, so the same movement is looked up once for all of Spotter rather than once per person. The thumbnail is loaded from Google's image host, and the clip, if you tap it, plays in a privacy-enhanced (no-cookie) YouTube player that is only added to the page once you tap and is removed again when you close the sheet. The link beside it opens YouTube's own search results.

Payments

In the native Android and iPhone apps, purchases are handled by Google Play or Apple. RevenueCat validates purchases and synchronizes subscription access with your Spotter account. RevenueCat receives your Spotter account ID, purchase history, product and transaction identifiers, subscription status, and technical information needed to process purchases. It does not receive your workout library or coaching conversations. Payment-card details stay with the store. See RevenueCat’s privacy policy.

Store subscriptions renew until cancelled in Google Play or Apple subscription settings. Deleting your Spotter account or uninstalling the app does not cancel store subscriptions. Cancel in the store first if you want billing to stop. Store providers may retain transaction records required for tax, accounting, fraud prevention, and legal obligations.

Where web purchases are offered, Spotter Plus is paid for through Stripe, and the payment happens on Stripe's own checkout page, not on Spotter's. Your card number, the security code on the back of it and whatever address your bank wants go to Stripe and stop there. Spotter does not store your payment-card details.

What Spotter keeps, on a row attached to your account, is only what it needs to know whether you are on the paid plan today:

  • the customer and subscription identifiers Stripe gave you, so the two systems can talk about the same person;
  • which plan it is, whether it is on trial, running, cancelled or unpaid, whether it is set to stop at the end of the period, and the date that period ends;
  • the date a payment last failed, if one has, so the app can say so and offer the fix;
  • a log of the notifications Stripe sends — the identifier and type of each one, and when it was handled. That log is how Spotter avoids acting on the same message twice. It holds no payment details.

Stripe is its own company and handles your payment data under its own privacy policy. Spotter sends it your account identifier so a payment can be matched back to you, and your email address if you give Stripe one at checkout.

If you cancel, the row stays until the period you paid for runs out and then says you are on the free plan. Deleting your account cancels a Stripe subscription first, then deletes your customer record at Stripe along with everything else. Stripe keeps its own record of the payments themselves for as long as tax and accounting law says it must — that part is not Spotter's to delete.

Connections

Spotter can connect to Strava, if you choose to, so that a workout you have just finished can be sent there as an activity. You connect it yourself in Settings → Connections, and nothing happens until you do.

The connection is one-way. Spotter writes to Strava and never reads from it: it does not see your activities, your friends, your routes, your segments or your profile, and no Strava data is ever given to any of the AI services above. What gets sent, and only when you tap Send to Strava on a session, is the workout's name, when you started it and how long it took, one line per exercise with the sets you logged, your total volume, any personal bests from that session, and a link back to Spotter.

When you connect, Strava gives Spotter's server two access keys. They are held on a row attached to your account on the server only — the app in your browser never receives them and cannot read them — together with your Strava athlete number and the identifier of each activity Spotter created, so the same session is never posted twice. Disconnecting deletes both keys and tells Strava to forget Spotter, and so does deleting your account. You can also revoke Spotter yourself at any time from your Strava settings.

Apple Watch, widgets and reminders

The Apple Watch companion exchanges the current workout and workout controls with your paired iPhone. If you grant Health access on the Watch, it can read live heart rate during an active session and keep the session running for workout controls and rest reminders. Heart-rate values stay on the Watch: Spotter does not send them to its server or AI providers. This version does not save a workout to Apple Health. You can manage Health permissions in your device's settings.

Widgets and Live Activities use a local shared container to display workout information, including on your Lock Screen or Dynamic Island when enabled. Anyone who can view those surfaces may see that information. You control widgets, Live Activities and notification previews in your device settings.

If you enable reminders, Spotter stores a push subscription or Apple push token, its association with your account, your timezone and reminder preferences, and delivery state. Apple's Push Notification service delivers iPhone notifications. You can turn reminders off in Spotter Settings or disable notifications in your device settings. Spotter does not use reminder tokens for advertising.

Operational measurements and creator codes

Spotter records usage allowances, AI request outcomes, processing times, errors and costs to operate the service, prevent abuse and improve reliability. Internal reports also summarize saves, completed workouts, use of features and subscription activity. These records can be linked to your account while it exists. RevenueCat also processes purchase information for subscription analytics.

If you enter a creator code, Spotter records which code you used and qualifying subscription transactions so it can apply the offer and account for creator earnings. Creator reporting shows aggregate counts and earnings rather than your workout library or coaching conversations. When you delete your account, the account link is removed from retained earnings records; transaction and payout records may remain for accounting.

Files and video reading

Uploads enter private temporary storage. Spotter's server sends supported audio or video to Google Gemini for transcription or workout extraction. Spotter attempts to delete its temporary file and Google's uploaded file after processing, including failed attempts. A cleanup sweep targets files left in Spotter storage after two hours; outages can delay cleanup. Extracted text and workout details remain on your saved card.

For public links with little written workout information, Spotter may also read available audio or video, automatically or when you request it. Source material fetched from a public post may contribute to the shared cache below. Your private uploads and supplied captions or HTML are excluded from new shared cache entries.

Deleting a temporary uploaded file does not erase a provider's separate processing or abuse monitoring records. Provider handling depends on the account and service configuration; see OpenAI's data controls and Google's API terms. In particular, Google's paid and unpaid services have different data-use terms.

Shared extraction cache

When a video is read for the first time, the resulting workout is cached under the video's public ID so the next person who saves the same video gets it instantly and no AI call is needed. That cache holds only information taken from the public post — never anything about who saved it. It is not linked to any account, so deleting an account does not remove it.

Taking your data with you

Settings → Data & privacy → Export my data writes everything you put into Spotter — your workout cards, every session you have logged, your plan, your collections, your chats with Pumpy and your settings — into a single JSON file on your device. It is a plain, structured, machine-readable format, so you can read it yourself or move it somewhere else. Your personal save key is deliberately left out of the file, because it works without a password and files get forwarded.

Deleting your account

Removing a workout deletes it immediately. To delete your whole account, open Spotter and go to Settings → Data & privacy → Delete account. You will be asked to type DELETE to confirm, and then the following are removed from the active app:

  • your workout cards, and any video files you uploaded;
  • every session you have logged, and your plan;
  • your collections and the corrections you made to cards;
  • your chats with Pumpy;
  • your profile, your settings and your personal save key;
  • your subscription record in Spotter; a Stripe subscription is cancelled and its customer record deleted. Google Play and Apple subscriptions must be cancelled in the store separately;
  • your Strava connection, if you made one — Strava is told to forget Spotter and both access keys are deleted;
  • your Apple sign-in authorization, if connected — Spotter asks Apple to revoke the stored grants and then deletes those tokens. You may need to sign in with Apple again first if the account was connected before token revocation was available;
  • the sign-in account itself.

Export your data first if you want to keep it. Account deletion cannot be undone in the app. Backup copies and provider security records may remain under their separate retention policies. Spotter keeps its own record of what the AI cost to run, and the deletion strips your account id out of those rows rather than keeping them attached to you. New AI reservation records are retained for up to 90 days; request admission records for up to 40 days, with scheduled cleanup.

If you cannot sign in, email business@quarterdeckcollective.com from your account's address and it will be erased for you.
