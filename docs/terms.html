What Spotter is

You save a link to a fitness video. Spotter reads what is publicly attached to that video — the caption, the description, and where those say nothing, the audio or the words on screen — and turns it into a workout card you can follow and log. It also gives you a weekly plan, a progress view, and a coach called Pumpy who can build workouts out of what you have saved.

Spotter does not host the videos. It links to them and shows the platforms' own players.

Your account

You need an account, and you must be at least 13 years old to have one — the same age Spotter's privacy policy is written around. One person, one account. Keep your password to yourself, and keep your personal save key private: it works without a password, which is the point of it and also the risk.

You can delete your account at any time from Settings → Data & privacy → Delete account. That erases your cards, sessions, plan, collections and chats, permanently, and cancels any subscription first.

How you may use Spotter

Save links you found yourself, for your own training. Do not scrape or automate the service, do not resell access to it, do not try to get around the limits on your plan, and do not use it to attack anyone or anything. Spotter's servers are small and shared, and the daily limits exist so one account cannot take the day away from everybody else.

Videos and the cards made from them

The videos belong to the creators who made them. Spotter's cards are derived from what those creators published publicly, and a card is a summary of exercises, not a copy of the video. You keep whatever you write yourself — your notes, your logs, your corrections.

If you are a creator and you want a card made from your video taken down, open an issue at the issues page saying which video it is, and it will be removed. That page is the contact point for copyright complaints until there is a formal one.

This is not medical advice

Spotter is training guidance, not medical advice, and neither Spotter nor Pumpy is a doctor, a physiotherapist or a coach who can see you. Exercises are read out of videos made by other people and may be wrong, unsuitable for you, or unsafe for how you train. If something hurts, stop, and see a professional. Talk to a doctor before starting a new programme, especially if you are injured, pregnant, or managing a condition. You lift at your own risk.
